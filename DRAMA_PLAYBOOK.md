## Page and series engine
Complete Page setup once and persist it. Each series gets its own folder and recurring fictional universe. Choose its continuing tension before committing to a season: who wants what, what prevents it, what choice repeatedly tests them, and what can change irreversibly?

## Episode construction

Confirmed user shorthand: “mag-experiment muna” requests a standalone short drama. Complete its central conflict through a consequential choice and payoff, then leave a brief unresolved implication, revealing final reaction or moral question that invites discussion. The ending may be pabitin emotionally, but must not withhold the main resolution or require another episode. Do not automatically attach it to an existing series. Prioritize a polished causal script, distinctive character voices and natural spoken Tagalog over forced twists, slogans or translated-sounding dialogue. This is a creative engagement goal, not a guaranteed performance result.

Example ending patterns for standalone experiments: the worker successfully sets a boundary, then the parent hesitates over an apology; the betrayed partner leaves after learning the truth, then the person who concealed it asks for forgiveness; a false accusation is corrected, but the wronged person declines the offered reconciliation. Each resolves the immediate conflict while leaving something meaningful to discuss. Examples are not approved canon.
Historical direction included short-reel experiments and later longer drama. Resolve the current format and selected series from the latest user request, project-state.json and registry; this playbook does not select an active series.

Within the opening 3–5 seconds, establish understandable conflict context: who is pressured, what they want or risk, and the immediate obstacle. Begin this as early as the scene allows; do not wait until second five to start the story. Sustain connected cause and effect through consequential dialogue, action and emotional changes. Plant evidence, make a choice and pay off a local beat. Serialized parts end with a cliffhanger that creates a specific next-episode question, not merely an ABANGAN label or follow request. Standalone experiments may end in a complete twist or consequence without promising an unplanned sequel.

A scene follows an event, not a sentence. Measure natural performance and plan the required scene ingredients and source durations under WORKFLOW_RULES.md R04–R06. Story scenes may contain several separately generated sources; do not force every scene into one 10-second setup.

## Tagalog dialogue
Use natural spoken Tagalog and relationship-appropriate terms of address. Avoid forcing formal translations or repeating what the image already shows. Separate direct dialogue, inner monologue, narration, action and silence. Exact approved lines remain unchanged in motion prompts.

## Dialogue-led pacing and provocative conflict

Read SHORT_DRAMA_EXAMPLE_BANK.md when selecting premises, writing scripts or planning direction. It contains selectable fictional examples, decision criteria, dialogue/action studies, fair twists and timed multishot coverage. Adapt to approved canon; do not mechanically combine its examples or treat them as performance evidence.

User direction recorded 2026-09-11: minimize dead air and prioritize consequential dialogue, emotion and readable facial expressions. Keep silent beats brief and motivated by a decision, revealing reaction, story evidence or meaningful physical action. Walking alone is not a reason to prolong a shot; trim travel unless it advances the scene. A held expression can carry the story when the viewer can read an emotional change.

Protect intelligible, natural delivery and the time needed to register an expression. Do not fill every second with speech, rush lines, add arbitrary movement or cut away from the emotional payoff merely to increase shot count. Bridge dialogue over purposeful action or inserts when appropriate. Review every pause: what changes or what does the viewer learn? Shorten or remove pauses with no clear function. Choose timing per scene rather than imposing an unsupported universal dialogue percentage.

Preferred premise options include infidelity, betrayal, oppression, family favoritism, class conflict, a poor protagonist, and concealed identity or someone pretending to be poor. These are options for original fictional stories, not automatic additions to approved series canon. Build controversy around specific conflicting wants, choices and consequences. Plant fair clues before a twist; explain a concealed identity's motive and practical plausibility. Avoid repeated humiliation without agency or payoff and arbitrary surprise wealth. Vary hooks and emotional intensity rather than defaulting to shouting.

Apply this direction to new drafts and production planning. Preserve approved dialogue and canon unless the user requests a revision. The user's observation about viral reels is creative reference, not verified evidence that these choices cause high views or retention.

## Dola AI Betrayal Engine & Storytelling Architecture (Scriptwriting-Only)

Creative concepts adapted from the Dola scriptwriting reference. Current image and video handoffs are listed in WORKFLOW_RULES.md; this creative section does not select providers.

### 1. The Counterpoint Betrayal Engine
One creative contrast is surface celebration colliding with a concealed personal loss, such as an anniversary toast exposing a secret. Its audience effect is a hypothesis until measured.
- **The Joyful Innocent:** Child, naive spouse, or trusting parent experiencing an untainted milestone (birthday, graduation, new keys). Sila ang nagbibigay ng matinding emotional warmth sa simula.
- **The Oblivious Anchor:** Family member or friend celebrating and reinforcing the illusion of family stability and security.
- **The Compromised Protagonist:** The character carrying the secret catastrophe (an affair, financial embezzlement, signed divorce petition, illegitimate child).

### 2. Optional 30-second writing study

This illustrative rhythm is not a minimum runtime, mandatory beat count or generation source specification.
```text
0.0–3.0s   | INSTANT HOOK        | The celebration or milestone is immediately established at peak joy.
3.0–8.0s   | THE INNOCENT BEAT   | The innocent character expresses adoration, gratitude, or achievement.
8.0–13.0s  | OBLIVIOUS REINFORCE | The anchor character celebrates, cementing the illusion of security.
13.0–18.0s | INTIMATE CONVERGE   | The protagonist steps in, hands and gaze meeting around the story prop.
18.0–20.0s | THE TACTILE TRIGGER | A physical sensory action (a clinking bell, key drop, glass clink, envelope slide).
20.0–25.0s | THE FACADE COLLAPSE | The protagonist’s smile dies; world muffles; camera isolates them.
25.0–30.0s | THE CLIFFHANGER     | Extreme portrait lock; single tear or stoic grief held into blackness.
```

### 3. The 5 Family Betrayal Archetypes
1. **The Forged Future:** Celebrating a child's milestone using funds secretly stolen or embezzled from family.
2. **The Stolen Milestone:** Elaborate anniversary party where one partner has already bought a condo and nursery for the other's sibling (*e.g., Lihim ng Kapatid*).
3. **The Blood-Money Inheritance:** Presenting a sick parent with medical funds acquired by selling ancestral land to their bitter rival.
4. **The Ghost Family:** Doting father playing the model family man while hiding texts and finances for a second family in another city.
5. **The Sacrificial Frame:** Sibling praised for corporate turnaround while secretly framing their other sibling for the criminal fallout.
